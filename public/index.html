About Wordy

What it is

A voice tutor for ten words that come up at work and in interviews. It introduces a word, then holds a conversation about it. You answer out loud, and you can cut in at any point to ask what the word means, ask for another example, or say next to move on.

How a turn works

Your voice goes through the browser's speech recognition and becomes text. The text goes to the tutor, a language model on Groq, which sends back two things: a spoken reply, and a verdict on your sentence. The verdict is one of correct, wrong sense, word missing, needs a full sentence, a question, or a skip. The reply is read aloud, and with hands-free on the mic opens again as soon as it finishes, so it runs like a phone call.

Who decides what

The language model writes the replies. The page keeps the score. It counts attempts, gives each word one retry, sends a missed word to the back of the queue for a second look, and decides when to move on. Keeping progress out of the model means a chatty or confused reply can never skip a word or mark one cleared by accident.

Going back to a word

Say “practise mitigate” or “let's do bottleneck again”, or tap any word in The set. The word you were on steps aside and comes straight back after, with no penalty. Practising a word you already cleared can't cost you anything: get it right and it stays cleared, miss it and it still stays cleared. After the set is finished, this runs a one-word session.

When the model is unavailable

If the tutor service does not answer within a few seconds, the page switches to a rule-based tutor that runs in the browser. It checks that the word was said, that there is a real sentence around it, and that the sentence sits near the right meaning. It is blunter than the model, but the lesson keeps going. The line above the conversation always shows which tutor is answering.

Why production, not recognition

Flashcards test whether you recognise a word. Using it inside a sentence you made up is what moves it into your active vocabulary, and the mistake that matters is not spelling. It is using a word confidently in the wrong sense, which is exactly what a text quiz cannot catch.

What a real version needs

A review schedule per learner so words return days later, not minutes. Word sets chosen by goal, such as interviews, sales calls or a specific exam. Pronunciation feedback. And a hosted speech model, because browser recognition is missing on Firefox and weak on low-end Android phones.

Browser support

Voice works in Chrome and Edge. Everywhere else, or if the microphone is blocked, you can type your answers and everything else behaves the same.
